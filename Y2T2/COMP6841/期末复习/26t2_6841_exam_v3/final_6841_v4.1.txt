## Question A4 [16 marks]

A startup has reached out to you, a CISO at a critical infrastructure energy company, on a cutting edge new solution for authentication in a new era with AI. Instead of conventional password methods, they are looking to create an AI authentication system that is able to authenticate users by conversing with them. This is through techniques such as asking questions such as what they were doing last on the device, monitoring their voice, facial recognition, typing patterns, and more. The system will tailor the authentication process to the user and their device, making it more secure and less prone to attacks.

Your company supplies energy to hundreds of thousands of households and businesses across the world and employs over 20,000 employees all across multiple timezones.

a) If a vote was cast, would you support the decision to mandate this technology into all employee mobile phones and laptops? (Yes or No)

NO

b) Justify your answer and provide any concerns. [7 marks] (guide: 150-250 words)

This presents significant privacy and legal risks. The system continuously collects highly sensitive biometric data from over 20,000 employees and hundreds of thousands of users worldwide, which could trigger various privacy-related legal issues in countries around the globe. Furthermore, "Biometrics is public evidence, not secret." once the database storing biometric information is leaked, the biometric information of employees and users will be permanently compromised and cannot be changed like passwords. If a user also enables biometric authentication elsewhere, attackers can easily use the leaked biometric information to bypass authentication. Moreover, since users and employees live in multiple time zones, employees may be unable to authenticate due to illness, poor lighting, injury, or other reasons. In critical infrastructure, authentication failure can disrupt normal work. Finally, using a biometric authentication system inevitably requires sourcing hardware and algorithms from other vendors, making supply chain attacks possible. If attackers control the upstream supply chain, they can implant backdoors in the hardware or software algorithms, easily breaching security defenses.
c) List and rank the top 3 recommendations you would you provide to the startup company that would make you more convinced to incorporate the technology in some way feasible. Within your response, state why these are the most important and why they are ordered this way. [9 marks]

1.
Recommendation (guide: 10-20 words): Biometric authentication should only be used as an enhancement to MFA, and should only be an option, not a requirement.
Reason (guide: 30-50 words): Using immature technologies as the sole authentication method is too risky. Mature, traditional authentication systems must be used as the primary authentication method, with biometric authentication only as one of the options to enhance MFA authentication.

2.
Recommendation (guide: 10-20 words): Biometric authentication must employ liveness detection and all biometric data must be stored on the user's local chip, not in the cloud.

Reason (guide: 30-50 words): Storing sensitive data in the cloud carries extremely high risks; a breach of the database could lead to irreversible data leaks. Liveness detection significantly increases the difficulty of presentation attacks.

3.
Recommendation (guide: 10-20 words): Provides a reliable backup authentication solution, ensuring that users can authenticate through other reliable means when biometric authentication is unavailable.
Reason (guide: 30-50 words): Biometric authentication may become unavailable for various reasons, such as user illness or adverse environmental conditions causing authentication failure. In such cases, reliable backup authentication methods must be provided to ensure system availability.

## Question B2 [14 marks]

A developer is utilising an API which cost is determined by the number of requests made. The API has a 64 character password to use that is unique. They accidently posted a screenshot of the password showing 39 of the characters (20 from the start and 19 from the end) in the internal company chat with 800 employees.

A co-worker notices the screenshot and reaches out to you on whether this is a concern.

a) Is this a concern?

this is not a concern

b) Why? Provide evidence to support your claim and state all assumptions. [8 marks] (guide: 250-350 words)
The original 64-character password had 39 characters leaked, significantly reducing the password space. However, considering that the remaining 25 characters might be a combination of numbers and uppercase/lowercase letters:

10 + 26 + 26 = 62^25 = 6.5 * 10^44

log_2(6.5 * 10^44) = 149

Therefore, a 25-character password is equivalent to a 149-bit binary password. According to NIST guidelines, passwords longer than 128 bits are absolutely impossible to brute-force with current human computing power, so this does not pose a problem.
